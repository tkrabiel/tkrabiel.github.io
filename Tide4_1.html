⬤ Higher ratio → more diurnal tide.

🔭 What you're seeing: Earth (tilted), Moon (inclined orbit), Sun (ecliptic plane). The tidal bulges shift off the equator, creating diurnal inequality. The 18.6‑year nodal cycle modulates the Moon's maximum declination.

🌊 How each factor creates constituents

  • Lunar orbit inclination (5.1°) → causes Moon's declination to vary ±28.6° over 18.6 years → modulates diurnal tides (O₁, K₁).
  • Earth's axial tilt (23.5°) → gives seasons and solar declination (±23.5°) → produces P₁, Ssa, Sa.
  • Nodal precession (18.6 yr) → changes the intersection of Moon's orbit with ecliptic → 18.6‑year modulation of all lunar tides (node factor).
  • Elliptic orbits → variation in distance → N₂, etc.
