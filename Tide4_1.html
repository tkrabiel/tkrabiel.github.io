📘 What is diurnal inequality?

When the Moon (or Sun) is not over the equator, its two tidal bulges are offset from the equatorial plane. As Earth rotates, a fixed observer passes through one large bulge and one smaller bulge → unequal high tides.

🧮 Key relationships:
  • Maximum diurnal inequality occurs when declination is maximum (±28.6° for Moon).
  • At the equator, the two high tides are equal even with declination.
  • At high latitudes, the inequality becomes extreme — can lead to only one high tide per day.

At 40°N with Moon declination 15°, the higher high is about 1.4× the lower high.

🌊 Tidal constituents from declination: K₁ (luni-solar), O₁ (lunar), P₁ (solar). Their amplitudes depend on the sine of twice the declination.
